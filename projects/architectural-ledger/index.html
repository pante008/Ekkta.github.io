01 — Problem Statement

Operations teams lose 40+ minutes per incident to fragmented tools

Analysts and managers juggle dashboards, ticket tools, and chat. Critical context is lost at handoff points and AI flags are dismissed when explanations are unclear.

15–40 minutes to form a hypothesis

Users spend about 60% of investigation time manually validating across tools instead of resolving anomalies.

3–5 tool context switches per incident

Dashboard → spreadsheet → Jira → Slack causes repeated rework and dropped context.

🔴 AI Trust Gap

Users dismiss AI alerts when lacking confidence score breakdown or cited data sources. In usability testing, 3 of 5 participants (60%) rated AI trust at 3/5 or below, citing missing "why" explanations and formula transparency as primary friction. One participant said: "I'm not sure what's happening because we jumped from confidence to interventions without explaining the root cause."

Overloaded interfaces

20+ metrics without hierarchy slow detection of critical anomalies.

Research Evidence

These insights come from 5 in-depth interviews conducted with operations, finance, and engineering professionals (2–10 years experience with analytics tools). Looppanel AI synthesized interview transcripts into themes, identifying context-switching as the #1 dominant pattern across all 5 users. Quantitative validation came from summative usability testing with 5 moderated remote sessions (April 2026), where the average task time revealed 60% of investigation time spent on manual context reassembly across tools.

Design Challenge

How might we design a single AI-powered interface that takes operations teams from alert → investigation → action → report → share in under 10 minutes, without ever leaving the dashboard?

03 — Design Approach / Methodology

12 total sessions across 3 evaluation phases · 23 hi-fi screens · 14 issues resolved

Process combined research-driven UX decisions with AI-accelerated synthesis and evaluation. Each phase used AI strategically: Looppanel auto-coded research, Stitch AI generated wireframes and hi-fi screens from structured prompts, Claude + Figma MCP surfaced cross-screen issues, Otter.ai transcribed usability sessions instantly, and Julius AI created charts from raw metrics. Human judgment remained essential for interaction risk assessment, missing feedback-state identification, and ethical interpretation of findings.

PHASE 2: LO-FI PROTOTYPE (2 Cognitive Walkthrough Sessions)

Stitch AI wireframes | 5-screen flow | Validated with 2 representative users

Low-fidelity screens generated from Master Prompt (Slack-inspired design system). Created 5-screen wireframe flow covering all 5 tasks: Monitor KPIs → Investigate Anomaly → Corrective Action → Generate Report → Share Findings. Conducted 2 cognitive walkthrough sessions with representative users to validate information architecture before hi-fi investment. Feedback informed 5 iteration cycles of lo-fi refinement.

PHASE 3: HEURISTIC EVALUATION (5 Evaluation Sessions)

23 Figma screens | Design system implemented | 14 issues identified (9 AI + 5 self-assessment)

High-fidelity prototype created in Figma (23 production-ready screens). Design system applied: Slack-inspired palette (#611F69 primary, #E01E5A critical, #2EBC4F success, #ECB22E warning). Dual-method evaluation: AI-assisted (Claude + Figma MCP reviewed all 23 screens, found cross-screen bugs like H-14 'oira' label and H-12 contradictory flows) + self-assessment (5 expert walkthrough sessions simulating end-to-end flows, caught interaction risks like H-04 dismiss without undo, H-09 rubber-stamp risk). Neither method alone captured the full picture—AI caught visual bugs, self-assessment caught interaction consequences.

PHASE 4: SUMMATIVE USABILITY TESTING (5 Moderated Remote Sessions)

5 participants | 25 tasks total (5 tasks × 5 participants) | Think-aloud protocol | Zoom + Otter.ai

April 3–4, 2026. Participants: 1 Senior Manager (10 yrs), 1 Finance Associate (4 yrs), 3 Developers (2–10 yrs)—all with analytics/BI tool experience, none with AI-powered dashboard experience. Sessions: 40–65 min each (average 52 min), Otter.ai auto-transcription. Metrics: 100% success rate (25/25 tasks), 8.89/10 usability score, 4.72/5 satisfaction, 0.28 avg errors/task. Verbatim feedback from transcripts mapped to 8 priority redesigns.

Design Decisions Cascade

All final screens reflect 14 heuristic evaluation issues + 8 usability testing redesigns. Key decisions: (1) Root cause surfaced as mandatory Step 3 to prevent investigation shortcuts; (2) post-sharing confirmation screen added to close feedback loop; (3) priority field changed from broken dropdown to explicit pill selector (Critical/High/Medium/Low) for clarity; (4) 3-step report flow (Generate → Preview → Send) enforces review before send.

06 — Usability Testing Insights

5 moderated sessions · 25 tasks · think-aloud · Zoom + Otter.ai

Remote moderated sessions with representative users from operations, finance, and engineering. Zoom recordings, Otter.ai transcripts, and post-session surveys.

Participant Recruitment & Demographics

All 5 participants were representative of the PRD target personas: 1 Senior Manager (10 yrs), 1 Finance Associate (4 yrs), 3 Developers (2–10 yrs experience). All used analytics/BI tools daily or weekly (Tableau, Looker, PowerBI, Observability tools). None had prior experience with AI-powered operations dashboards, making them ideal for testing novel features like AI-generated investigation flows and confidence scores.

Test Protocol Details — Duration: 40–65 minutes per session (average 52 min). Recording: Zoom + audio capture (MP4 files). Transcription: Otter.ai auto-transcription with AI Chat for extraction. Think-aloud: non-directive moderation; participants verbalized decisions without moderator interruption (except for clarification). Data capture: timestamps extracted from Zoom timecode, errors recorded in real-time, assistance requests logged by moderator, post-task satisfaction scores (1–5 Likert).

Research Questions Driving Test Design — Can users complete all 5 tasks without external tools? How quickly can users identify critical anomalies and understand root cause? Do AI confidence scores and recommendation explanations increase trust and reduce hesitation? Where do users struggle with the investigation flow, and what feedback states are missing? Can users successfully share findings across multiple platforms with a single gesture?

Success Criteria — Task success ≥95% (target: 100%); usability score ≥7.0/10 (target: 8.89/10 achieved); avg task time within ±1.5× protocol targets; satisfaction ≥4.0/5 (target: 4.72/5 achieved).

Cross-Participant Themes

08 — AI Tools Throughout

6 AI tools · every phase · ~20 hours of workflow accelerated

End-to-end experiment in AI-assisted UX. Each tool served a specific, deliberate role — AI accelerated without replacing critical design judgment.

AI-Assisted Workflow Impact

Six AI tools were integrated across the full design lifecycle (research → prototyping → evaluation → testing → analysis → visualization). Total AI acceleration: ~20 hours of manual labor reduced to ~4 hours of AI-guided work.

Research: Looppanel auto-coded 5 interviews → 51 user needs in 45 min. Prototyping: Stitch AI generated 23 hi-fi screens from structured prompts. Evaluation: Claude + Figma MCP reviewed 23 screens for cross-screen consistency. Testing: Otter.ai transcribed 5 sessions instantly. Analysis: Claude calculated usability score and outliers. Visualization: Julius AI created 4 publication-quality charts in 15 min.

What AI Missed — and Why It Matters — H-04 (Dismiss without Undo), H-09 (Rubber-Stamp Risk), and H-10 (Missing Deep-Link) were interaction issues that only surfaced when simulating actual use under task pressure.

Conclusion: AI is an accelerant, not a replacement. It improved speed, breadth, and consistency, but the final product decisions still required human design judgment and ethical review.

09 — Key Takeaways & Next Improvements

What designing an AI product with AI tools taught me

AI evaluation + self-assessment are complementary

AI caught cross-screen consistency bugs (H-14 'oira' label, H-12 contradictory report flows) in minutes—work that would take hours manually. Self-assessment caught interaction consequences AI cannot predict (H-04 dismiss without undo, H-09 rubber-stamp forms). Alone, neither method found the complete 14-issue picture. Recommendation: run AI evaluation first to establish baseline, then self-assessment to catch interaction gaps.

Transparency is the prerequisite for AI trust

AI trust satisfaction was the lowest score (3.80/5 vs 4.72/5 overall). Users weren't distrusting AI — they wanted to understand it. Three of five participants requested confidence formula breakdown and data-source visibility. Redesign U-05 adds an expandable confidence panel.

One outlier tells the most important story

P4's Task 4 time of 13.5 minutes looked like statistical noise (4.5× over target). Otter.ai transcript analysis revealed it was actually two stories: external interruption plus a design gap (missing post-sharing confirmation). Qual + quant together prevented misinterpretation and protected the right redesign decision.

The PRD is your north star — keep returning

Both "Not Met" requirements were in the PRD from day one. They only surfaced under real usability test conditions — not expert heuristic evaluation. Real users navigating under task pressure exposed what expert review missed.

Precise prompts produce precise outputs

Vague Stitch AI prompts produced generic screens. Prompts that named specific Figma screen IDs, listed exact component states, and referenced PRD requirements produced production-quality UI. Prompting is a design discipline.

Every research hour compounds forward

The 51 user needs from research shaped 55 PRD requirements, which shaped 23 screens, which were tested and validated. The quality of the final product was determined in the first two weeks of user research.

Quant + qual together prevents blind spots

Outlier metrics alone were insufficient until supported by transcript evidence.

AI is strongest in acceleration, not accountability

AI improved speed and breadth; final product decisions required design judgment and ethical review.
